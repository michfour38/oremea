# Resonance seed boundaries

Canonical working boundaries for participant-facing Resonance seed questions. Room audits add room-specific distinctions; these rules apply across all rooms.

## Job of a seed

A seed collects lived material. It does not perform the Daily Mirror's interpretation for the participant.

Prefer actions, cues, actual moments, choices, sequences, concrete contrasts, and differences the participant can point to. The Mirror can do the pattern recognition and synthesis later.

## Evidence boundary

- Begin close to ordinary lived experience rather than psychological, coaching, academic, or therapeutic language.
- Do not seed an emotion, motive, diagnosis, hidden cause, worth claim, identity claim, trauma story, attachment story, virtue, deficit, or causal explanation into the premise.
- A contrast may offer a foothold; it must not decide what the participant is supposed to discover.
- Questions must tolerate `IDK`, `nothing`, `both`, `depends`, humour, contradiction, correction, a short answer, or rejection of the premise.

## First-read clarity boundary

A participant should understand the literal question on the first read. If a tired person has to reread it to work out what the sentence is asking, rewrite it.

- Prefer ordinary subject → action → object speech: `What do they do?`, `What happens next?`, `What changed?`, `How could you tell?`
- Contrast should make the question easier to enter, not make the participant mentally reverse the sentence before answering.
- Avoid backwards absence logic, double negatives, nested conditions, abstract noun chains, and compressed conceptual phrasing.
- Avoid making a simple action sound theoretical: prefer `someone stops doing it` over `care becomes visible through absence`; prefer `who does what` over `distribution of relational labour` in participant-facing copy.
- Use metaphor only when it is everyday speech and instantly clear. If the metaphor carries the meaning instead of illuminating it, use the literal action.
- One thread question should have one main movement. A deeper exercise may contain several steps when the steps are explicit and easy to follow.
- Read the question aloud. If the natural response is `what?`, the wording has failed even if the underlying construct is sound.

## Tone boundary

Resonance is curious, exact, human, spacious, and quietly incisive. It sounds like a perceptive person asking a real question, not a therapist, researcher, course writer, or model demonstrating intelligence.

Layman speech is the default. Depth comes from the evidence collected and what becomes visible across it, not from complicated wording.

## Cross-room audit checks

Every seed should eventually be compared across rooms for:

1. construct overlap
2. evidence form
3. perspective
4. time orientation
5. contrast structure
6. interpretation load
7. premise risk
8. answer-range tolerance
9. Mirror yield
10. participation yield
11. first-read clarity
