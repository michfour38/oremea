# Resonance seed audit — The Bearing

Internal working record for later cross-room comparison. This file does not drive participant-facing content.

## Room boundary

- Room: The Bearing
- Theme: Values, priorities, trade-offs, and integrity
- Seed job: collect what the participant's choices, allocations, trade-offs, decision boundaries, and corrections show about what matters now; do not tell them what they value or what the right hierarchy should be
- Voice: plain, human, concrete, first-read clear
- Movement: notice what choices show → see what gets priority → observe pressure decisions → locate decision ownership → compare words and actions → correct drift → name a present direction
- Evidence preference: recent choices, actual allocations of time/attention/resources, explicit trade-offs, decision examples, participant-stated priorities, observable follow-through, chosen corrections
- Answer tolerance: a useful seed must still work with short answers, uncertainty, `nothing`, `both`, `depends`, humour, contradiction, correction, or rejection of the premise
- Interpretation boundary: do not treat one choice as proof of identity, moral character, true values, hidden motive, selfishness, virtue, courage, fear, integrity, or lack of integrity
- First-read clarity boundary: values language must stay attached to an ordinary choice, cost, priority, rule, decision, or action. If a participant has to decode `authority`, `alignment`, `congruence`, `orientation`, or `trade-off` before answering, rewrite it in lived speech.

## Boundaries with previous rooms

- **The Hearth** asks where and with whom participation becomes easier or harder.
- **The Mirror** asks what the participant repeatedly does inside interaction and what tends to happen next.
- **The Garden** asks what care and support cost, restore, distribute, request, and require to remain workable.
- **The Bearing** asks what the participant's real choices show about what gets priority, what is being traded away, who gets to decide, and whether actions are moving in the direction they say matters.

Potential overlap is intentional only when the evidence target differs:

- Time, energy, and resources belong in **Garden** when the target is capacity, care, labour, support, rest, or sustainability.
- Time, energy, and resources belong in **Bearing** when the target is priority, allocation, trade-off, or what a choice reveals about what matters.
- Repeated behaviour belongs in **Mirror** when the target is a recurring interaction pattern; it belongs in **Bearing** only when the target is repeated follow-through or repeated exception against a stated priority.
- Choice language belongs in **Hearth** when the target is approach, distance, fit, or belonging; it belongs in **Bearing** when the target is priority and decision ownership.

## Seven-day data arc

| Day | Main data being collected | Cross-section dimensions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Values visible in choices | recent decisions, stated importance, allocation evidence, competing priorities, accepted consequence |
| 2 | Priority in real allocation | spare capacity destination, what gets less, deliberate delay, named costs, priority/evidence comparison |
| 3 | Choices under pressure | fast-choice priority, short-term/long-term contrast, slowing conditions, accepted cost, pressured-decision review |
| 4 | Decision ownership | own decisions, approval-seeking if present, influence without final say, mutual authority, live decision map |
| 5 | Words and actions | existing match, repeated exceptions, protecting standard, smallest correction, current mismatch map |
| 6 | Drift and realignment | early drift cue, repair, unrealistic commitment, visible next action, review plan |
| 7 | Integration | clearer priority, deliberate reduction elsewhere, available decision, future priority check, participant-authored bearing |

## Cross-room comparison notes after four rooms

- **Hearth / Bearing overlap:** both contain choice language. Hearth uses choice to show approach, fit, distance, or participation in relationship. Bearing uses choice to show priority, trade-off, decision ownership, and direction.
- **Mirror / Bearing overlap:** both can examine repeated actions. Mirror asks what pattern repeats and what interaction follows. Bearing asks whether repeated action supports or contradicts what the participant says matters.
- **Garden / Bearing overlap:** both use time, energy, attention, money/resources, responsibility, and structure. Garden asks whether care/support is workable and how capacity circulates. Bearing asks what receives priority and what gets less as a result.
- **Bearing centre of gravity:** choices reveal current priority only as evidence for inquiry, not as proof of the participant's identity or `true values`.
- **Distinct Bearing evidence forms introduced:** competing-priority maps, opportunity cost, short-term/long-term choice contrast, accepted consequence, decision-authority maps, words/actions comparison, repeated-exception review, drift/repair/review plans.
- **Watch in later rooms:** attraction, triggers, conflict, and future-design rooms should not use `what matters`, `priority`, or `alignment` as generic depth language. If the real target is what gets chosen, deferred, protected, or given final authority, it probably belongs here.

## Cross-room comparison fields to retain

Compare every seed on at least these axes:

1. **Construct overlap** — is another room collecting the same thing under different language?
2. **Evidence form** — cue, behaviour, episode, preference, distinction, choice, sequence, allocation, consequence, participant-stated meaning.
3. **Perspective** — self, other person, shared decision, external condition, resource allocation.
4. **Time orientation** — recent choice, current allocation, pressured episode, live decision, recurring commitment, future review.
5. **Contrast structure** — whether the question offers a useful foothold without deciding the answer.
6. **Interpretation load** — how much meaning the participant must manufacture before they can answer.
7. **Premise risk** — whether the wording assumes a value hierarchy, motive, virtue, deficit, moral failure, courage, selfishness, integrity, or causal story.
8. **Answer-range tolerance** — whether `IDK`, `nothing`, `both`, `depends`, a correction, or a short concrete answer remains valid data.
9. **Mirror yield** — whether the answer gives observable decisions, allocations, costs, and follow-through rather than polished values language.
10. **Participation yield** — whether the question gives the participant something they can choose, delay, protect, decline, revise, repair, or review without prescribing the answer.
11. **First-read clarity** — whether a tired participant can understand the literal question immediately without translating abstract values language first.
