# WORKS integrity doctrine

WORKS should prevent uncertainty from being disguised, complexity from being dumped on the buyer, and access from being abused by either side.

## Product guardrails

1. Unknown remains unknown until evidence supports a stronger claim.
2. A known hard mismatch is not eligible for provider outreach.
3. Paid plans buy workflow and demand tools, never suitability or ranking.
4. Evidence has provenance and freshness; stale or expired evidence cannot be treated as current confirmation.
5. Regulated credentials require appropriate authority-backed evidence before WORKS presents them as verified.
6. Buyer outreach is qualified and bounded; WORKS is not a lead-spray system.
7. Contact details and commercial requirements are disclosed only as part of a buyer-requested provider interaction, and only to the extent required by the buyer's chosen contact method.
8. A provider enquiry is for fit and current capability; detailed formulation, design, samples, engineering, testing or other development work must be scoped between the parties rather than extracted as unpaid quote work.
9. Reviews attach to a real WORKS interaction and cannot be used simply to punish a provider for declining work.
10. A multi-step production route remains multi-step; WORKS does not force one provider to cover unrelated work.
11. A route with missing providers becomes a sourcing request, not a fabricated match or dead end.
12. Provider responses and direct commercial relationships belong to the buyer and provider; WORKS facilitates rather than traps the relationship.
13. Acquisition and conversion are attributable so lead loss and marketing performance can be measured without changing match integrity.
14. Claims such as certification, licensing, safety, compliance or regulated status cannot be promoted from self-report to verified without appropriate evidence.
15. All public and outreach language distinguishes possible fit from guarantee, current evidence from provider report, and platform discovery from appointment or warranty.
