# Resonance seed audit — The Pulse

Internal working record for cross-room comparison. This file does not drive participant-facing content.

## Room boundary

- Room: The Pulse
- Theme: Desire, attraction, and movement
- Seed job: collect what catches attention, what desire feels like, how it changes with time and reality, and what movement the participant chooses; do not tell them what attraction means about them
- Voice: plain, human, concrete, first-read clear
- Movement: notice pull → locate signals → compare recurring and changing wants → slow it down → test it → choose with consequence → name the next movement
- Answer tolerance: short answers, uncertainty, `nothing`, `both`, `depends`, humour, contradiction, correction, and rejection of the premise all remain usable data
- Interpretation boundary: Do not turn attraction into proof of compatibility, destiny, unmet need, wound, attachment pattern, sexuality, identity, or hidden motive. Body cues are optional participant evidence, not truth detectors.

## Boundaries with earlier rooms

- **The Hearth** — conditions around belonging, welcome, fit, and relational participation.
- **The Mirror** — the participant's recurring move inside interaction and what tends to happen next.
- **The Garden** — care, support, labour, capacity, resources, receiving, asking, and sustainability.
- **The Bearing** — priority, trade-off, decision ownership, words/actions, and chosen direction.

## Seven-day data arc

| Day | Main data being collected | Cross-section dimensions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Initial pull | attention capture, approach cue, interest growth, first move, recent attraction episode |
| 2 | Body and attention | body cue, interest/distance contrast, pace/focus change, waiting, sensation-to-action sequence |
| 3 | Familiar and changing wants | recurring attraction, changed attraction, imagined gain, object/experience distinction, current desire evidence |
| 4 | Time and reality | speed, information loss, desire revision, useful pace, desire-over-time sequence |
| 5 | Small movement | action/information/time distinction, acknowledged desire, reversible step, learning, real-world test |
| 6 | Yes, no, and consequence | clear yes, chosen no, cost decision, reality constraint, current choice map |
| 7 | Integration | recurring pull, clarified desire, reduced attraction, chosen direction, participant-authored next step |

## Cross-room overlap notes

- **Hearth / Pulse:** both can involve moving closer or away. Hearth asks whether a space or person supports belonging; Pulse asks what attracts attention and desire before any belonging claim is made.
- **Bearing / Pulse:** both end in choice. Bearing asks what gets priority; Pulse asks what the participant wants and what reality/consequence does to that wanting.
- **Garden / Pulse:** energy language belongs to Garden when the target is capacity or replenishment; it belongs to Pulse only when the participant uses it to describe interest or attraction.
- Distinct Pulse evidence: first pull, optional body signals, attraction over time, imagined gain versus known facts, reversible tests, wanted-but-declined choices.
