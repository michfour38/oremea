# Resonance seed cross-room map

Working comparison after the ten-room seed upgrade. This is an internal design record, not participant-facing copy.

## Centres of gravity

| Room | Primary job | Evidence it should uniquely favour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Hearth | Belonging, welcome, fit, relational participation | arrival cues, room-making, approach/distance, self-adjustment, belonging contrasts |
| The Mirror | Recurring self-participation inside interaction | interaction loops, event/meaning separation, recurring roles, counterexamples, choice points |
| The Garden | Care, support, capacity, labour and resource circulation | receiving effects, giving costs, invisible work, handover, requests, sustainable arrangements |
| The Bearing | Priority, trade-off, decision ownership and integrity | allocation, opportunity cost, pressured choices, decision authority, words/actions, correction |
| The Pulse | Desire, attraction, interest and chosen movement | first pull, optional body cues, attraction over time, imagined gain, reversible tests, wanted-but-declined choices |
| The Shadow | Strong reactions under pressure and updated choice | reaction onset, familiar pressure move, then/now comparison, participant-stated aim, usefulness/cost, alternative response |
| The Forge | Conflict, responsibility, repair and distance | tension onset, listening, known/unknown accounts, responsibility split, repair request, repair evidence, thresholds |
| The Vision | Concrete future shared-life design | ordinary-day scene, rhythms, agreements, responsibility backup, decision rules, resource scale, prototypes |
| The Gathering | Integration without forced unity | connection/non-connection, two truths, through-lines, sorting, complexity-preserving story |
| The Becoming | Repeated lived continuation | visible action, frequency, cues, environment, low-capacity version, keepable agreement, return after lapse |

## Highest-risk overlaps

- **Hearth ↔ Pulse:** closer/distance language. Hearth asks whether participation feels possible; Pulse asks what attracts interest.
- **Mirror ↔ Shadow:** recurrence. Mirror studies ordinary interaction loops; Shadow studies marked reactions under pressure.
- **Mirror ↔ Forge:** sequences. Forge earns its place only when the sequence leads into responsibility, repair, or distance.
- **Garden ↔ Vision:** resources/responsibility. Garden audits current circulation; Vision designs a future system.
- **Garden ↔ Becoming:** capacity. Garden asks how care/resources move; Becoming scales one chosen practice to current capacity.
- **Bearing ↔ Vision:** principles/priorities. Bearing studies current allocation and trade-off; Vision uses principles to organise future design.
- **Bearing ↔ Becoming:** follow-through. Bearing compares words and actions around priority; Becoming builds repetition around one chosen action.
- **Pulse ↔ Shadow:** body/attention cues. Pulse = wanting; Shadow = marked reaction. Neither treats body sensation as proof.
- **Shadow ↔ Forge:** pressure. Shadow = internal reaction and choice; Forge = relational conflict and repair.
- **Gathering ↔ everything:** Gathering must integrate without quietly stealing another room's job or manufacturing one grand theme.

## Global seed boundaries confirmed

1. **Seeds collect life; Mirrors interpret.** Seed questions favour observable moments, cues, actions, choices, sequences, contrasts and participant-supplied meaning.
2. **First-read clarity is a hard gate.** If a tired participant's natural response is `what?`, rewrite the question.
3. **Contrast is an invitation, not a conclusion.** It should make the question easier to enter and preserve `both`, `neither`, `depends`, or correction as valid answers.
4. **No hidden-construct smuggling.** A seed may not introduce trauma, attachment, worth, motive, fear, protection, identity, nervous-system claims, compatibility, destiny, integrity, harm, or causation unless the participant supplied the construct.
5. **Generated material is not participant evidence.** Prior Mirrors and AI questions may support continuity but cannot prove something about the participant.
6. **Several pieces may stay separate.** Recurrence comes before meaning; changed wording comes before significance; proximity is not causation.
7. **Every room stands alone.** No participant-facing seed may require completion of another Resonance room or assume a prior insight exists.
8. **Participation remains available.** A strong seed ultimately gives the participant something they can notice, test, choose, ask, stop, revise, build, repair, leave, or continue without prescribing the answer.

## Cross-sectional fields

Every room audit now retains at least: construct overlap, evidence form, perspective, time orientation, contrast structure, interpretation load, premise risk, answer-range tolerance, Mirror yield, participation yield, first-read clarity, and room-independence risk.

## Coverage check after all ten rooms

The current set collects evidence across: external cues, internal cues, body signals without treating them as truth, behaviour, speech, attention, preferences, concrete episodes, repeated sequences, roles, allocation, resource flow, requests, boundaries, decision ownership, desire, pressure responses, conflict, repair, future system design, integration, repeated practice, low-capacity adaptation, and return after lapse.

The main global failure mode to keep testing in live use is **question language becoming more sophisticated than the lived thing it is trying to ask about**. The first-read clarity gate remains active even after this seed pass.
