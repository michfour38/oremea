# Resonance seed audit — The Shadow

Internal working record for cross-room comparison. This file does not drive participant-facing content.

## Room boundary

- Room: The Shadow
- Theme: Internal protection and defensive strategies
- Seed job: collect strong reactions, familiar moves under pressure, past/present similarities, participant-stated aims, costs, and updated choices; do not assume a defence mechanism or hidden wound
- Voice: plain, human, concrete, first-read clear
- Movement: notice reaction → see familiar move → compare past and present → name what matters → test usefulness and cost → choose an updated response → keep choice with the participant
- Answer tolerance: short answers, uncertainty, `nothing`, `both`, `depends`, humour, contradiction, correction, and rejection of the premise all remain usable data
- Interpretation boundary: Do not call a response protective, traumatic, defensive, triggered, wounded, fearful, or nervous-system driven unless the participant supplies that language. Earlier experience may be compared with current experience; it may not be declared the cause.

## Boundaries with earlier rooms

- **The Hearth** — conditions around belonging, welcome, fit, and relational participation.
- **The Mirror** — the participant's recurring move inside interaction and what tends to happen next.
- **The Garden** — care, support, labour, capacity, resources, receiving, asking, and sustainability.
- **The Bearing** — priority, trade-off, decision ownership, words/actions, and chosen direction.
- **The Pulse** — attraction, interest, desire, reality-testing, and chosen movement.

## Seven-day data arc

| Day | Main data being collected | Cross-section dimensions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Strong reaction | first change, attention lock, first urge, recognition timing, reaction sequence |
| 2 | Familiar move under pressure | habitual response, immediate usefulness, lost information, aftermath, tool/cost map |
| 3 | Past and present | older recurrence, earlier conditions, surface similarity, present difference, then/now comparison |
| 4 | What matters in the moment | participant-stated importance, intended effect, naming importance, alternative choice, event-to-choice map |
| 5 | Usefulness and cost | current usefulness, cost, lost participation, fit/proportion, cross-context comparison |
| 6 | Updated response | new ability, staying present, lower-cost response, trust in alternative, current response plan |
| 7 | Integration | recognition, participant-stated purpose, retained function, reclaimed choice, participant-authored check |

## Cross-room overlap notes

- **Mirror / Shadow:** both can show recurrence. Mirror maps ordinary interaction loops; Shadow focuses on strong reactions under pressure and what choice becomes available once the reaction is noticed.
- **Pulse / Shadow:** both may collect body or attention cues. Pulse ties them to wanting/interest; Shadow ties them to a marked change in reaction. Neither treats a body cue as a truth detector.
- **Bearing / Shadow:** both may ask what matters in a moment. Bearing tests priority through choices; Shadow asks the participant what feels important during a strong reaction without declaring a hidden value or wound.
- Distinct Shadow evidence: recognition timing, immediate usefulness/cost of a pressure move, then/now comparison without causal claims, fit/proportion across settings, updated response plan.
