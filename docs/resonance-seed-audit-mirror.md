# Resonance seed audit — The Mirror

Internal working record for later cross-room comparison. This file does not drive participant-facing content.

## Room boundary

- Room: The Mirror
- Theme: Patterns, roles, and relational self-awareness
- Seed job: collect observable repetitions, sequences, interpretations, participation choices, and changes; do not tell the participant what a pattern means
- Voice: plain, human, concrete, recognisable
- Movement: notice what I do → place it in sequence → separate event from interpretation → see my contribution → locate choice
- Evidence preference: repeated actions, actual interaction sequences, first reads, observable consequences, participant-stated intentions, specific counterexamples, chosen experiments
- Answer tolerance: a useful seed must still work with short answers, uncertainty, `nothing`, `depends`, humour, contradiction, correction, or rejection of the premise
- Interpretation boundary: recurrence does not prove motive, identity, attachment style, trauma response, defence mechanism, emotional state, or hidden cause

## Boundary with The Hearth

The Hearth asks **where and with whom participation becomes easier or harder**. The Mirror asks **what the participant repeatedly does inside interaction and what tends to happen next**.

Potential overlap is intentional only where a different cross-section is being collected. A conversational question belongs here when the target is the participant's repeated move, sequence, interpretation, contribution, or choice—not belonging, welcome, fit, or relational safety.

## Seven-day data arc

| Day | Main data being collected | Cross-section dimensions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Automatic participation and recurring social jobs | automatic behaviour, attention target, expectation change, effect on other, concrete sequence |
| 2 | Repeating interaction loops | trigger moment, own move, other's move, ending, observable sequence |
| 3 | Event versus interpretation | first meaning, observable facts, confirmation/challenge attention, action after interpretation, event-to-action sequence |
| 4 | Recurring roles across relationships | repeated labour, intended outcome, displaced possibilities, counterexample, comparison |
| 5 | Own contribution without absorbing the whole dynamic | repeated contribution, intention/impact distinction, feedback reception, responsibility boundary, shared-system map |
| 6 | Choice points and experiments | earliest choice point, small variation, early cue, evidence of durable change, planned experiment |
| 7 | Integration | clearest recurrence, evidence across examples, next-step sequence, owned choice, participant-authored pattern statement |

## Cross-room comparison notes after two rooms

- **Hearth / Mirror overlap:** both may collect conversational behaviour. Hearth uses it as evidence of welcome, fit, room-making, and belonging; Mirror uses it as evidence of the participant's recurring move and sequence.
- **Hearth centre of gravity:** environmental and relational conditions around participation.
- **Mirror centre of gravity:** recurrent self-participation inside interaction.
- **Distinct Mirror evidence forms introduced:** interaction loops, interpretation-versus-event separation, cross-relationship role recurrence, intention/impact comparison, distributed contribution maps, counterexamples, choice-point experiments.
- **Watch in later rooms:** avoid reusing `what do you do / what happens next` without a new evidence target. Sequence language is a method, not sufficient distinction by itself.

## Cross-room comparison fields to retain

Compare every seed on at least these axes:

1. **Construct overlap** — is another room collecting the same thing under different language?
2. **Evidence form** — cue, behaviour, episode, preference, distinction, choice, sequence, outcome, participant-stated meaning.
3. **Perspective** — self, other person, group/space, mutual interaction.
4. **Time orientation** — present/general, recent episode, remembered pattern, current decision, future participation.
5. **Contrast structure** — whether the question offers a useful foothold without deciding the answer.
6. **Interpretation load** — how much meaning the participant must manufacture before they can answer.
7. **Premise risk** — whether the wording assumes an emotion, motive, identity, injury, virtue, deficit, or causal story.
8. **Answer-range tolerance** — whether `IDK`, `nothing`, `both`, a correction, or a short concrete answer remains valid data.
9. **Mirror yield** — whether the answer is likely to give the Daily Mirror observable material rather than polished self-theory.
10. **Participation yield** — whether the question ultimately gives the participant something they can notice, choose, do, approach, or leave alone without prescribing the answer.
