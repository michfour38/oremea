# Resonance seed audit — The Hearth

Internal working record for later cross-room comparison. This file does not drive participant-facing content.

## Room boundary

- Room: The Hearth
- Theme: Belonging & relational safety
- Seed job: collect lived material; do not do the Daily Mirror's interpretation for it
- Voice: plain, human, concrete, inviting
- Movement: recognisable contrast → observable detail → participation
- Evidence preference: actions, cues, actual moments, choices, differences the participant can point to
- Answer tolerance: a useful seed must still work with short answers, uncertainty, `nothing`, `both`, humour, contradiction, or a participant rejecting the premise
- Interpretation boundary: do not seed an emotion, motive, diagnosis, hidden cause, worth claim, or identity claim into the premise
- First-read clarity boundary: the literal question must make sense on the first read; contrast may invite the answer but must never require mental reversal, abstract translation, or rereading to discover what is being asked

## Seven-day data arc

| Day | Main data being collected | Cross-section dimensions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Arrival and reading a new space | external cues, approach/withdrawal, expression, self-adjustment, concrete episode |
| 2 | Being included, heard and known | inclusion cues, attention, self-editing, room-making, understood/misunderstood contrast |
| 3 | Sharing conversational space | pace, dominance, self-presence, silence, conversational reciprocity |
| 4 | Choice, no and disagreement | receiving limits, giving limits, disagreement, freedom to participate, boundary outcome |
| 5 | Mutual contribution | welcome cues, maintenance effort, two-way curiosity, giving/receiving, shared labour |
| 6 | Fit and relational discernment | continued participation, distance cues, unfamiliarity/fit contrast, own contribution, evidence-based choice |
| 7 | Integration into future participation | low-effort self-expression, context contrast, behaviour contrast, earlier noticing, chosen creation |

## Cross-room comparison fields to retain

When later rooms are audited, compare every seed on at least these axes:

1. **Construct overlap** — is another room collecting the same thing under different language?
2. **Evidence form** — cue, behaviour, episode, preference, distinction, choice, sequence, outcome, participant-stated meaning.
3. **Perspective** — self, other person, group/space, mutual interaction.
4. **Time orientation** — present/general, recent episode, remembered pattern, current decision, future participation.
5. **Contrast structure** — whether the question offers a useful foothold without deciding the answer.
6. **Interpretation load** — how much meaning the participant must manufacture before they can answer.
7. **Premise risk** — whether the wording assumes an emotion, motive, identity, injury, virtue, deficit, or causal story.
8. **Answer-range tolerance** — whether `IDK`, `nothing`, `both`, a correction, or a short concrete answer remains valid data.
9. **Mirror yield** — whether the answer is likely to give the Daily Mirror observable material rather than polished self-theory.
10. **Participation yield** — whether the question ultimately gives the participant something they can notice, choose, do, approach, or leave alone without prescribing the answer.
11. **First-read clarity** — whether the literal question is immediately understandable without mentally reversing absence, unpacking abstract nouns, or rereading the sentence.
