# Resonance seed audit — The Vision

Internal working record for cross-room comparison. This file does not drive participant-facing content.

## Room boundary

- Room: The Vision
- Theme: Conscious creation and relationship architecture
- Seed job: collect a participant's concrete design for a future shared life, including rhythms, responsibility, decisions, resources and tests; do not let design language drift into generic values or care analysis
- Voice: plain, human, concrete, first-read clear
- Movement: picture ordinary life → name rhythms → assign responsibilities → design decisions → count resources → test assumptions → build a usable blueprint
- Answer tolerance: short answers, uncertainty, `nothing`, `both`, `depends`, humour, contradiction, correction, and rejection of the premise all remain usable data
- Interpretation boundary: Future design may be aspirational, but questions should keep returning to ordinary days, who does what, what is available, and what can be tested. Do not require another person's agreement where it has not been given.

## Boundaries with earlier rooms

- **The Hearth** — conditions around belonging, welcome, fit, and relational participation.
- **The Mirror** — the participant's recurring move inside interaction and what tends to happen next.
- **The Garden** — care, support, labour, capacity, resources, receiving, asking, and sustainability.
- **The Bearing** — priority, trade-off, decision ownership, words/actions, and chosen direction.
- **The Pulse** — attraction, interest, desire, reality-testing, and chosen movement.
- **The Shadow** — strong reactions under pressure, familiar responses, past/present comparison, and updated choice.
- **The Forge** — conflict events, responsibility, repair, evidence, and relational distance.

## Cross-room overlap notes

- **Garden / Vision:** both can involve responsibility, resources, and routines. Garden audits how current care actually moves; Vision designs a future shared system and tests whether the design works.
- **Bearing / Vision:** Bearing examines current priorities and trade-offs; Vision uses chosen principles only to organise a concrete future design.
- **Forge / Vision:** both ask about disagreement and decision rules. Forge responds to actual conflict; Vision designs how future disagreement and authority should be handled before conflict occurs.
- Distinct Vision evidence: ordinary-day future scene, explicit agreements, responsibility backup, decision categories, current-resource-limited scale, untested assumptions, prototypes.
